# Product Manager Interview

A generic, structured Product Manager interview practice skill for PMs preparing for their next product career move.

Version 0.1.0

## 60-second start

1. Choose the role you are targeting: Associate PM, Product Manager, Senior PM, or Principal PM.
2. Share your CV or résumé so the practice can reflect your experience, achievements, and gaps. Redact sensitive personal details first.
3. Choose a Product Manager case study to practise.
4. Ask the assistant to run the interview, probe your decisions, and give structured feedback against the assessment criteria.

The goal is simple: practise realistic case studies that connect your past experience to the product role you want next.

## Who it is for

This skill is designed for PM candidates seeking their next product opportunity, including:

- Associate Product Managers
- Product Managers
- Senior Product Managers
- Principal Product Managers

## Supported interview format

The primary supported format is Product Manager case-study practice, including:

- Product design and product sense
- Execution, prioritisation, and trade-offs
- Metrics and measurement
- Product strategy
- Follow-up probing and structured assessment

## CV personalisation

The assistant should understand your CV or résumé before starting practice. It uses that context to tailor prompts, probe relevant experience, identify likely gaps, and calibrate the discussion to your target role.

Do not include information you are not comfortable sharing with your chosen AI assistant.

## What it does

This tool provides realistic PM interview practice and coaching across case-study questions, assessment criteria, follow-up probing, and improvement feedback.

## Privacy

No personal CVs, résumés, or user profiles are included in this repository. Users should share only the career information they are comfortable providing to their chosen AI assistant and should redact sensitive personal details where appropriate.

## Scope

This is general Product Manager interview preparation. It is not an official hiring rubric or representation of any company's interview process.
