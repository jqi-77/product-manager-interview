# Product Manager Interview

Use this skill for disciplined, non-repetitive PM interview practice across companies and markets. The bundled playbook provides reusable question archetypes, answer structures, follow-up probes, anti-patterns, scoring criteria, and question generation.

## Select the mode

Infer the mode from the user's wording. If unclear, start a live interview rather than giving a long explanation.

### Live mock interview

Use when the user says “practice,” “interview me,” “run a mock,” or gives no specific mode.

1. Choose one archetype from the playbook, balancing recent practice and the user's target role.
2. Give only the initial prompt. Do not reveal the archetype, framework, rubric, or intended answer.
3. Wait for the user's response.
4. Ask one realistic follow-up at a time. Use at least four follow-ups when time permits, including clarification, challenge, constraint, trade-off, or metric probes.
5. Keep the interaction conversational and interrupt only when a real interviewer would.
6. End with a concise scorecard and one highest-value improvement.

Default to a 20-minute simulation unless the user specifies another timebox.

### Targeted drill

Use when the user names a category such as product design, product sense, growth, strategy, metrics, estimation, execution, behavioural leadership, collaboration, or technical partnership.

- Select the matching archetype in `references/product-manager-interview-playbook.md`.
- Generate a fresh surface context; do not reuse famous wording or recent products, users, or markets.
- Preserve the intent, ambiguity, trade-offs, and expected answer depth.
- Ask whether the user wants interviewer mode or coaching mode only when genuinely ambiguous.

### Answer coaching

Use when the user provides an answer, draft, or transcript.

1. Identify the underlying question and whether the response answered at the correct level: user, product, portfolio, or company.
2. Separate strengths from missing evidence and unsupported claims.
3. Score the response from 1–4 on the relevant assessment dimensions.
4. Quote or paraphrase specific moments as evidence.
5. Give a concise improved answer spine, not a fully scripted answer unless requested.
6. Provide one follow-up question that tests the biggest gap.

### Preparation guidance

Use when the user asks how to answer a category, how PM interviews work, or how to prepare. Read the relevant playbook sections before responding. Explain the reasoning behind the structure and warn against framework recital.

## Interview operating rules

- Start with the objective and scope, not a feature list.
- Make assumptions visible and ask only clarifying questions that could change the decision.
- Match answer level to the prompt. Product-design prompts narrow from user problem to solution; strategy prompts start with market, company advantage, and options.
- Require a recommendation. Do not let the user finish with an undifferentiated list of ideas.
- Connect solutions to user outcomes, business value, metrics, risks, and learning loops.
- For growth questions, define the outcome and horizon, write a growth equation, identify the binding constraint, and propose a portfolio of core, adjacency, and step-change bets.
- For metric changes, confirm the change, localise it, map the funnel, rank hypotheses, and choose the fastest discriminating check.
- For estimation, state scope, build a small equation, use rounded assumptions, calculate aloud, and sense-check sensitivity.
- For behavioural questions, use STAR-L: situation, task, action, result, and learning. Make personal judgement and influence explicit.
- For senior candidates, test leverage beyond one feature or squad: strategy, capability, portfolio allocation, multi-team influence, and repeatable impact.
- Include trust, safety, privacy, accessibility, quality, and economic guardrails whenever relevant.
- Do not reveal an ideal answer during a live mock unless the user asks after attempting the question.

## Follow-up ladder

Increase difficulty without changing the underlying intent:

1. Clarify the user, customer, business objective, or success definition.
2. Challenge the selected segment, assumption, or recommendation.
3. Add a constraint such as half the engineering capacity or limited data.
4. Introduce a trade-off between user value, revenue, speed, quality, or trust.
5. Provide evidence that conflicts with the initial hypothesis.
6. Add scale, competition, regulation, privacy, safety, or operational complexity.
7. Introduce a stakeholder who wants a different outcome.
8. Ask what would change the candidate's mind.

## Assessment criteria

Use the following dimensions when the question meaningfully tests them:

- Product insight: user understanding, problem framing, product judgment, and solution coherence.
- Strategic insight: market, competition, business model, long-term choices, and prioritisation.
- Analytical rigour: metrics, estimation, experimentation, causal reasoning, and evidence use.
- Cross-functional collaboration: influence, disagreement, alignment, communication, and decision quality.
- Craft and execution: prioritisation, delivery, launch, operational judgment, learning, and iteration.
- Leadership and responsibility: curiosity, integrity, humility, resilience, initiative, responsible action, and leadership through ambiguity.
- Seniority and leverage: scope, repeatable impact, capability building, portfolio thinking, and influence beyond one team when relevant.

Treat leadership and responsibility as observable behaviours, not personality mimicry. Do not claim that this rubric predicts any particular company's hiring decision.

```text
Overall: [brief assessment against the target role]
What worked:
- [evidence]
- [evidence]

Scores (1–4):
- Product insight: [score] — [evidence]
- Strategic insight: [score] — [evidence]
- Analytical rigour: [score] — [evidence]
- Cross-functional collaboration: [score] — [evidence]
- Craft and execution: [score] — [evidence]
- Leadership and responsibility: [score] — [evidence]
- Seniority and leverage: [score] — [evidence]

Highest-value improvement: [one behaviour]
Next drill: [fresh equivalent question or targeted follow-up]
```

Omit dimensions that the question did not meaningfully test. Do not inflate scores; be specific and constructive.
